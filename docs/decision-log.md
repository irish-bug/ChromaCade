# Decision Log — ChromaCade

A record of paths considered and rejected, and why — so nobody (human or AI) re-proposes a dead end without knowing it was already tried and ruled out. Ordered roughly chronologically. When in doubt about "why don't we just do X the normal way," check here first.

## Platform / compute
- **Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W, not Pico or Teensy (for unit #1).** Considered a Pico (cheaper, instant-on, better battery life, native ADC) and a Teensy (purpose-built Audio Library for real-time polyphonic synthesis). Rejected for the first unit because the Pi Zero's Linux + `pygame.mixer` gives polyphony/mixing essentially for free in Python, and the LiPo battery already provides hours of runtime, so the Pico's power/instant-on advantages weren't worth losing that simplicity. **Still open:** a later unit (#2-4) may be built Pico-based as a deliberate experiment once the Pi Zero version proves the design out. Don't "helpfully" suggest switching the whole project to Pico/Teensy — this was already weighed and decided.
- **Python + `pygame.mixer`, not a custom audio engine.** Chosen specifically for effectively-free polyphony (multiple simultaneous mixer channels) without hand-rolling a synth engine. Do not suggest rewriting this in a "more proper" audio framework unless the Pico/Teensy path is actually being pursued — that tradeoff was already made deliberately.
- **Sharp/flat handled as a semitone-offset shift, not note-renaming.** Deliberately sidesteps enharmonic naming edge cases (is it B# or C?) that a naive "rename the note" implementation would hit. This was an early, deliberate architecture decision — don't reintroduce note-renaming logic.
- **Direct GPIO wiring for the 7 note buttons, not a button matrix.** Matrix wiring would introduce ghosting on certain multi-key combinations, which breaks chord support — a core feature. Direct wiring costs more GPIO pins but the Pi Zero has plenty, so there was no real reason to matrix.

## Power
- **4x AAA batteries — rejected outright.** Raw AAA voltage (6.4V fresh alkaline, or 4.8V and dropping for NiMH) doesn't reliably hit the Pi's required regulated 5V in either chemistry — alkaline runs too high, NiMH runs too low/brownout-prone. Don't suggest AAA-based power again.
- **TP4056 alone — insufficient.** It's a charger only (raw battery voltage out, 3.0-4.2V), with no boost stage — would never give the Pi a stable 5V. Needs a charge+boost combo board, which is what got sourced (DWEII Type-C 5V/2A boost/charge/protection board).
- **Bare LiPo + separate boost module** was considered as a two-part alternative to an integrated charge+boost board, but the integrated board (charges via USB-C AND regulates to 5V AND has an optional switch pad, all on one board) won on simplicity/fewer failure points.
- **Unit #1 bring-up: skipping the LiPo/DWEII boost-charge board for now, powering via micro-USB cable passed through the existing back-panel hole instead — decided 2026-08-13.** Cable secured at the pass-through so handling/movement doesn't tug on internal connections. This is a bring-up simplification, not a reversal of the battery decision above — battery integration is still the plan for the finished unit. Consequence while powered this way: table-top, supervised use only, not a standalone/portable unit.

## Controls
- **Trackball (Centipede-style) for pitch bend — rejected.** Two independent reasons: (1) cost, too expensive for a single part relative to the rest of the BOM; (2) behaviorally wrong anyway — a trackball reports *relative* motion with no spring-return-to-center, while pitch bend wants *absolute* position that snaps back to zero on release. The KY-023 joystick module does this natively and is far cheaper. Don't re-suggest a trackball for pitch bend specifically (it could still be fun as a totally different, unrelated control on a future/premium unit, but not for this job).
- **MTS-103 toggle switch for flat/natural/sharp — considered, then superseded by a round 3-way rocker switch (XINYIELE ON-OFF-ON).** Both are electrically identical (3-position SPDT); the rocker was chosen because pressing is an easier motion for toddler hands than the toggle's pinch-and-flip. If you see MTS-103 referenced in older material, the rocker is the actual current part.
- **12mm tactile buttons with 7-color caps (TWTADE) — rejected and returned.** Too small, and 3 of the 7 "colors" were white/black/gray, not usable rainbow colors for the note-color mapping. Replaced with 14mm MX-style clicky mechanical switches (Outemu Blue) + separately-sourced rainbow PBT keycaps (Elacgap, 8 usable colors). Don't re-suggest small tactile buttons as a simplification — the size and color-fidelity were both dealbreakers, not preferences.
- **Custom 3D-printed keycaps with color filament — considered, then rejected.** The blocker wasn't the print itself (cheap, ~$1 of filament for a full set) but the need to buy a full spool (~$20) per color to get the range needed, which added up across colors even though usage-per-spool would be tiny. Pre-colored PBT keycaps (Elacgap) solved this without any filament investment. If cost of colored filament sampler packs ever drops or is already on hand, custom caps remain a viable path for shape customization (finger-scoop sculpting etc.) — but it's not the current plan.
- **32-piece small PCB variety pack — rejected and returned.** Individual boards too small once the panel/shelf layout was finalized. Replaced with 15×20cm (6"×8") double-sided perfboard.
- **Octave encoder's built-in push-button: was never dead — retest confirmed 2026-08-14, "confirmed dead" call reversed.** Briefly treated as a dead switch on 2026-07-29 (rotation/quadrature always worked fine; only the click failed to register, on GPIO25). Reopened same day once it was noted that GPIO25 (physical pin 22) had prior solder/desolder history from the now-disassembled 3-key test mount's "Key 3" — a plausible fault independent of the switch, matching this project's dominant pattern (marginal/damaged connections, not defective parts). Retested on GPIO8, a genuinely untouched spare: clean registration, no double-triggers. The switch was fine all along; GPIO25's pad was the actual fault. **Button now permanently on GPIO8** (see `gpio-pin-assignments.md`). The font encoder's push-button is no longer the sole physical click control on this build — the octave side has a real, working click again, so its function (still unassigned — see `open-questions.md`) is a live question again, not a moot one.

## LED / color feedback
- **Per-button individual NeoPixels — considered, then superseded.** Original plan was one discrete through-hole WS2812 LED next to each of the 7 note buttons. Superseded by a single WS2812 7-LED ring mounted near the OLED, for much simpler wiring (one chain, one location) instead of 7 distributed pixels.
- **Ring as 7 fixed per-note positions — considered, then superseded.** An intermediate idea had each of the ring's 7 LEDs permanently assigned to one letter-note (so the ring would need a printed legend mapping position to letter). Superseded by the simpler final design: all 7 LEDs light the *same* unified color together, representing whichever note/chord is currently active — no legend needed, and the code is simpler (set one color, not 7 addresses).
- **Full 360° rainbow hue spread for the 7 letters — likely wrong, not yet finalized.** Blending colors from opposite sides of the hue wheel washes toward gray/brown. Current direction is compressing the 7 letters into a narrower hue arc (~180-250° candidate) so chords blend into vivid "color chords" instead. This is still an open prototyping task (see open-questions.md) — don't default to evenly-spaced full-rainbow hues without running the blend-test script first.
- **Interior NeoPixel strip for whole-case backlighting — planned 2026-07-21, length chosen 2026-07-24 (16 LEDs).** Unit #1 is printed in translucent PLA, so an interior WS2812 strip lights the whole case shell, not just the ring near the OLED. Intended for Learn-mode/Simon-mode visual feedback (in addition to the ring's unified note-color display) and general kid appeal. Since each pixel is individually addressable regardless of chain topology, ring and strip are not forced to always show the same pattern — the "alternate, never lit simultaneously" behavior described for Simon/Tutor mode is a UX choice, not a wiring constraint.
  - **Chaining plan superseded 2026-07-28: ring and strip are now two independent chains, not one GPIO12 chain.** Originally planned to chain off the ring's spare OUT triad onto the same GPIO12 data line (no new GPIO needed, 23 pixels total on one chain). Changed after the combined chain showed persistent color corruption on bring-up (multi-color instead of clean fills, only a handful of strip pixels lighting) that survived resoldering the ring's DIN joint — splitting them isolates whether the ring or the strip (or both) is the actual problem, and shortens each individual run. Strip now gets its own DIN on GPIO13 (PWM1, a separate hardware channel from the ring's GPIO12/PWM0) — see `gpio-pin-assignments.md`. This pin was freed by moving the octave encoder's push-button (previously GPIO13, functionally unused — see `open-questions.md`) to GPIO25. The original ~25-LED / ~300mA shared-envelope power budget math (`hardware-bom.md`) was computed assuming both amps near-max; with the second amp since removed (see "Audio hardware" below), actual headroom is now larger than that figure, not smaller — the combined 23-pixel total pixel count is unchanged even though the two chains are wired separately.

## Case geometry
- **Flat, table-parallel control surface — considered, then rejected in favor of an angled arcade-cabinet profile.** A flat top would foreshorten the OLED/button view from a toddler looking straight down, and would put his hands/forearms over his own sightline. The 45° angled panel (arcade-cabinet-style) was chosen for better sightline and because the angle creates useful interior wedge volume behind the panel for components.
- **Case shape evolved through several iterations** — plain wedge → wedge + front wall → front wall + flat shelf → final profile (continuous base, ~3" front wall with speakers, ~2" tilted shelf with all 4 shelf controls, ~2.5" angled panel with just notes+OLED+ring, back wall for power/charging). The panel shrank (4" → 2.5") and the shelf grew (1" → 2") specifically because the toggle switch and font encoder moved off the panel onto the shelf. If you see references to a 4" panel or 1" shelf in older notes, those are superseded — case-design.md has the current numbers.
- **Shelf control placement flipped once, deliberately, based on hand-workflow reasoning:** font encoder was originally planned centered on the shelf, then moved to the far right (paired with the pitch joystick) once the two-handed workflow was articulated — left hand does setup (octave, then accidental) before playing, right hand does live shaping (font, then pitch bend) while notes are held. The MTS/rocker switch similarly moved from an unspecified/general position to explicitly beside the octave encoder on the far left, for the same reason.
- **Power switch relocated from the side panel to the back wall, next to the USB-C charging port.** Originally planned side-mounted (matching the volume pot's deliberate-friction placement). Overridden after further thought about keeping it out of a toddler's reach during play — grouping it with the charging port keeps both "parent-only" power-path controls together and out of reach. `control-layout.md` and `design-philosophy.md` are updated to match (2026-07-19); don't move it back to the side panel as a "consistency" fix.
- **Speaker grilles: landscape stadium (pill) hex-hole pattern cut directly into the front wall — not a plain rectangle, not a separate printable insert.** The validated design uses a portrait-native `stadium_hex_grill(25.4, 38.1)` module rotated 90° at the cut site so it sits landscape on the landscape speaker enclosure (69.9×34.9mm cone housing). A same-day cross-tool editing session (Claude usage was capped, so `antigravity-cli` was used for a few changes) garbled this: the intent had been a separate rectangular *test plate* that contained the stadium shape (so it could print flat while the housing printed upright), but it came out as an actual rectangular grille cutout with a separate screwed+glued insert part, plus unrelated regressions elsewhere (floating mounting bosses, reverted rocker/joystick dimensions). Restored 2026-07-19: hex holes cut straight into the front wall, no separate insert (fewer failure points, nothing pry-off-able, matches the chunky/durable philosophy) — `chromacade-speaker-grilles.scad` was deleted. Don't reintroduce a rectangular grille or a separate insert part; neither was an intentional design choice.
- **Housing and back panel kept as separate, fully-independent `.scad` files, each with its own duplicated dimension constants — not `include`/`use`-linked modules, and not a single monolithic file with boolean export toggles.** Both alternatives were tried and rejected same-day: an `include`/`use`-based split (a shared config file plus housing/back-panel/grille-insert modules, OOP-style dependencies) broke the actual workflow of pasting raw code directly into unsaved OpenSCAD windows on a separate machine; a monolithic single file with `export_housing`/`export_back_panel`/`export_grille_inserts` boolean toggles was tried as a middle ground and added its own confusion. Current state (standalone files, Printables/Thingiverse-style, safe to copy-paste or download independently) is intentional — don't re-propose linking them or consolidating into a toggle file. Accepted tradeoff: shared constants (`case_w`, `wall`, `boss_x`, etc.) are duplicated across files and must be updated in lockstep by hand — confirmed 2026-07-19 they were still in alignment despite this.
- **KNOWN BUG, not yet fixed in CAD (found 2026-07-22): speaker body depth was never modeled against the shelf's right-side components, causing a real physical interference.** `hardware_cutouts()` only cuts the front-wall grille *opening* for each speaker (`spk_cx = ±45`) — it never modeled the physical speaker unit's front-to-back depth as a 3D volume intruding into the shelf's interior cavity. On unit #1 this meant the right speaker (Shutao 3W/8Ω, JST-PH2.0, "69.9×34.9mm cone housing" per `hardware-bom.md` — that figure is the front-face footprint, not confirmed depth) physically collides with the right-side shelf cluster: font encoder sits at shelf x=-35, joystick at shelf x=-65 (more outboard, closer to the speaker), both behind/above the speaker's grille position at front-wall x=-45. Left side (rocker at shelf x=70, octave encoder at x=45) wasn't reported as a problem, only needing a sideways rocker orientation + trimmed encoder mounting corners — milder because nothing shelf-side sits as far outboard as x=-65 does on the right. **Unit #1 workaround (shipping-deadline fix, not a CAD change):** disassembled/trimmed the right speaker by hand to reduce its depth, confirmed sound quality still acceptable, regained clearance for the joystick. **For the next print:** get an actual caliper depth measurement on the speaker before attempting a CAD fix (repositioning the joystick's shelf x-position further from the speaker, locally recessing the shelf underside near x=-65, or increasing shelf depth are the candidate approaches — none chosen yet, see open-questions.md). Don't assume the current shelf/speaker coordinates already have adequate clearance; they don't.

## Menu / mode interaction
- **Plain long-press on G (or A) alone to enter/exit the menu — rejected.** Toddlers routinely hold single notes just to sustain the sound, so a plain long-press is not a safe, low-false-positive gesture. Revised to a two-handed modifier combo: hold the font encoder's push-button *and* long-press G to enter, hold it *and* long-press A to exit. This is the current, correct design — don't simplify back to a plain long-press as a "cleaner" UX suggestion.
- **"Musical Simon" clarified as two distinct modes, not one.** The person's initial phrase evoked classic Simon Says (memory/reproduce-a-growing-sequence), but the actual described behavior was tutor/follow-along (light the next note, wait for the correct press, no memorization). Both are worth building; tutor mode is the priority/default for a toddler, Simon mode is lower-priority and more suited to an older child.

## Software setup
- **`dtoverlay=hifiberry-dac`, not `dtoverlay=max98357a`, for the MAX98357A amps.** The Pi-tutorial-standard `max98357a` overlay drives SD_MODE on a GPIO (GPIO4 by default, overridable via `sdmode-pin=` but not worth depending on remembering that override every time `config.txt` is touched) — GPIO4 is the locked note-button-A pin (`gpio-pin-assignments.md`). `hifiberry-dac` is a generic I2S DAC overlay with no SD_MODE control; SD_MODE is instead handled purely in hardware (a resistor to VIN — see `chromacade-hardware-test-plan-2026-07-20.md`), keeping the question off the GPIO budget entirely. Don't switch back to `max98357a` as the "proper" overlay for this chip — it works, but only by claiming a pin this build can't spare.
- **`/etc/asound.conf` (system-wide), not `~/.asoundrc`, for the ALSA default device — set by card name (`sndrpihifiberry`), not index.** The eventual app runs as a systemd service, which may not reliably see a per-user `$HOME`, so a per-user `~/.asoundrc` risks silently not applying. Naming by card name instead of index also survives card-index shifts across reboots/units.
- **No venv — global Python packages via `apt`.** This is a single-purpose device running one app forever, so per-project isolation buys nothing; going through `apt` also sidesteps Bookworm's PEP 668 pip restriction and is simpler to replicate identically across the planned 4-unit build. Don't suggest a venv/pip-based setup as a "best practice" cleanup — the tradeoff was deliberate for this project's specific single-app, multi-unit-replication shape.
- **For the pip-only packages `apt` doesn't have (pyfluidsynth, several adafruit-circuitpython-* libs), always install with `sudo pip3 install --break-system-packages`, never a plain per-user `pip3 install`.** Found 2026-08-15: `pip3 install pyfluidsynth --break-system-packages` as a regular user installs into that user's own package directory, invisible to `sudo python3` (root has a separate environment/`$HOME`) — and the LED ring genuinely needs root for its PWM/DMA access (real `/dev/mem` requirement, not a permissions oversight; no safe group-based workaround exists for that specific mechanism, unlike gpiozero's plain GPIO reads). Discovered the hard way when `adafruit-circuitpython-ads1x15` and `adafruit-circuitpython-ssd1306`/`-framebuf` were installed for the user but not root. Installing system-wide via sudo keeps root and the regular user's Python environments identical, avoiding this class of bug recurring as more pip-only dependencies get added.

## Sourcing / hardware verification
- **ADS1115 over MCP3008** for the analog-to-digital converter, despite MCP3008 being the more commonly-referenced Pi ADC in generic tutorials — ADS1115 was in stock, is I2C (shares the bus with the OLED and LED ring rather than needing separate SPI pins), and has higher resolution (16-bit vs 10-bit). Don't suggest switching back to MCP3008 as a "more standard" choice.
- **8Ω speakers confirmed compatible with the MAX98357A amp**, despite 4Ω being the more commonly-cited "ideal" impedance for that chip — 8Ω is within its supported range, and since the speakers themselves are only rated 3W anyway, the reduced power delivery at 8Ω isn't a practical limitation.
- **Speaker JST-PH2.0 connectors don't plug into the MAX98357A's screw terminals** — this is a known, accepted mismatch, resolved by stripping/screwing bare wire ends rather than expecting a plug-and-play connector match. Not a sign something was sourced wrong.
- **Second MAX98357A amp removed from unit #1 as of 2026-07-28 — one amp now drives both speakers.** Originally "one amp per speaker channel" (`hardware-bom.md`), motivated by the amp being a mono Class-D chip. The second amp had been unconfirmed/suspect since the amp-wiring saga (see hardware bring-up notes) and was deliberately deferred rather than urgently chased. Removing it reduces total 5V current draw on the shared power feed — real headroom gained for the other 5V devices (LED ring/strip), see the LED-chain-splitting entry above. If audio quality/volume with a single amp driving both speakers turns out insufficient, revisit rather than assuming this is final.
